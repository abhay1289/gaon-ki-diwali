Five nights of light

A walk through Diwali, one evening at a time. Light the lamps, draw at the doorway and fill the sky.

Scroll to begin

On the new-moon night of Kartik, the darkest of the autumn, homes across India and around the world answer the dark with rows of small clay lamps.

Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and many Buddhists keep the festival, each with their own story. What they share is the light.

Day 01

Dhanteras

धनतेरस

The festival opens with prosperity. Houses are swept and washed, and families buy something new in gold, silver or brass, even a single spoon, for luck. At dusk a lamp goes at the threshold for Lakshmi and for Dhanvantari, physician of the gods.

      Day 02

      Choti Diwali

      नरक चतुर्दशी

      Krishna defeats the demon Narakasura and frees the people he held captive. Before dawn there is an oil bath, and by evening the first rows of diyas line the windowsills.

      Day 03

      Lakshmi Puja

      लक्ष्मी पूजा

      The main night. Rama comes home to Ayodhya, and Lakshmi walks the streets looking for the brightest, cleanest homes. At the door, a rangoli of coloured powder welcomes her in.

      Day 04

      Govardhan Puja

      गोवर्धन पूजा

      Krishna lifts Govardhan hill on one finger to shelter the villagers from Indra's storm. In thanks, temples build Annakut, a mountain of food, often counted as chhappan bhog: fifty-six offerings. In Gujarat it is also the new year.

      Day 05

      Bhai Dooj

      भाई दूज

      Yama visits his sister Yamuna, and she welcomes him with a tilak and a meal. Sisters and brothers do the same today, and the festival closes the way it began: at home, with family.
